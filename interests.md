## Do climate models get intense storms right? (postdoc)

Before using climate models to study future storm risk, you need to know if they produce realistic intense storms. My approach looks, at every grid point, at the most intense low-pressure systems, and describes them with three parameters: peak, tendency and asymmetry. I used it to evaluate 27 CMIP6 global models and a regional climate model over North America (1980–2014).

**Why it matters for risk:** a hazard model built on climate simulations is only as good as the storms those simulations produce.

## Storms, extreme rain and wind in a warmer climate (postdoc)

Extratropical cyclones bring a large part of the extreme rain and wind at mid-latitudes. I followed these storms in 35 years of hourly data, measured the extreme rain and wind (above the 99th percentile) within 1000 km of each storm centre, and split them into two parts: how often extremes happen, and how strong they are. I then compared historical and future regional climate simulations (CRCM6-GEM5) over North America.

**Why it matters for risk:** insurers think about hazard in terms of how often and how strong. This split shows whether a future change comes from more events or from stronger ones.
