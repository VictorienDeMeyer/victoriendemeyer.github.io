Weather has always fascinated me. As a child, I was obsessed with how water could fall from the sky, why wind even existed, or where thunder could come from.

As I grew older, I discovered phenomena I could never witness from my own backyard, like hurricanes and tornadoes, and my curiosity only grew. I also came to understand climate change, and how these extremes are becoming an ever greater threat to societies. For as long as I can remember, I have wanted to study atmospheric extremes, and that is what led me to where I am today.

After studying theoretical physics at the University of Toulouse, France, I did a CNRS-funded PhD on how tropical convective systems and the extreme rainfall they bring respond to changes in surface temperature. During my postdoc at UQAM in Montreal, Canada, I focused on the strong winds and heavy rain brought by severe midlatitude storms, and on how they could affect key industries in a warming climate.

I have always wanted my work to be useful beyond research papers, so I chose a postdoc built around an industry partnership to prepare my transition to the private sector. Now I’d like to go one step further and work in insurance, consulting, energy or other operational settings. For example, the hazard side of catastrophe models, the part that describes the storms themselves, is a challenge I’m particularly interested in.

I’m a European (French) citizen and a Canadian permanent resident, happy to move anywhere in the EU, North America or Asia for an exciting project.
